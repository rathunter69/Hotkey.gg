Lesson 2: Workbook, sheets and cells

Foundations · How Excel works · easy · Free

An Excel file is a workbook, and each tab along the bottom is a worksheet: a grid of cells, each named by its column letter and row number, such as C4. In this lesson you jump around the Weekly Sales Report and its Costs sheet with Go To and the sheet keys. Every formula you will write points at cells this way, and a real model spreads them across several sheets.

What you will do

  1. A cell reference is its column letter then its row number, and Go To (Ctrl+G) jumps straight to one: type the reference and press Enter. Go to C4, the Tuesday Units, with Go To.
  2. A range is a block of cells written corner to corner, B3:B7, and Go To selects one the same way. Select the Sales figures B3:B7 with Go To.
  3. A workbook is the file and each worksheet is a tab along the bottom: Ctrl+PgDn moves to the next sheet, Ctrl+PgUp to the previous one. Move to the Costs sheet.
  4. Come back to the Sales sheet.
  5. A reference can name its sheet first, Costs!B3, and Go To follows it onto that sheet. Jump straight to the Monday cost, B3 on the Costs sheet, with Go To.
