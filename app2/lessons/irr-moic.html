Lesson 208: Returns: IRR and MOIC

Valuation · LBO · hard · Paid

The sponsor puts equity in at entry and takes equity out at exit: exit enterprise value (11.0x FY31 EBITDA) less the debt still outstanding. MOIC is equity out over equity in; IRR is the annual return that turns one into the other over five years. It’s the house from the DCF chapter again: bought with a small down payment and a large mortgage, the same rise in value is a far bigger return on the down payment. Build both, and read them against the 20% a sponsor needs. The key is IRR.

What you will do

  1. The sponsor sells after five years at the exit multiple on FY31 adjusted EBITDA, repays whatever debt is left, and keeps the rest. That equity at the exit is the whole payoff. Exit enterprise value, net debt at the exit and equity at the exit in C102:C104.
  2. MOIC is money out over money in. Entry equity counts the owners’ rolled stake beside the sponsor’s cheque, because both hold the new company on the same terms. Equity at entry, the sponsor’s and the rolled stake together, in C105, and MOIC in C106.
  3. IRR is the annual rate that turns the money in into the money out over the hold. The counter puts the exit in the year the hold names, so a four-year hold moves it. The equity cash flows in C107:H107, in at closing and out in the hold’s last year, and IRR on them in C108.
  4. IRR by hand in C109 with RRI over the hold, and the difference from IRR in C110, reading zero.
  5. Each holder gets its share of every cash flow, so the sponsor and the owners earn the deal’s rate exactly. A check reading zero proves the split and the return rows are wired the same way. The split in C111:C116: the sponsor’s share, both holders’ cash flows, their IRRs and the check between them.
  6. The lender is out the loan at closing and gets interest and repayments back each year, the balance at the exit. Its IRR near the loan’s rate is the proof that no repayment is missing from the row. The senior lender’s row in C117:H117 and its IRR in C118, which lands close to the loan’s 8%.
  7. The flag in C119: Clears when IRR is at least the 20% hurdle, Short when it isn’t.
  8. Does it tie? Watch the exit multiple go to 12x: IRR and MOIC both rise, and the flag reads them.
