Lesson 23: Fonts, fills, borders

Foundations · Format · easy · Free

undefined

What you will do

  1. Ctrl+B makes the selection bold, Ctrl+I italic and Ctrl+U underlined; on a mixed selection they follow the active cell, so start from a plain cell and every cell goes on. The title in A1 is plain text: make it bold with Ctrl+B so the page has a heading.
  2. Alt, H, F, G grows the font a step; Alt, H, F, K shrinks it. Everything else on the page stays one size. The title being bigger is what makes it the title. One font, one size, and the title is the one exception: take A1 one size up with Increase Font Size, Alt, H, F, G.
  3. Shift+Space, then Ctrl+B: a whole row dressed in two presses. Formatting a row or a column whole is faster than selecting the cells, and it catches the ones you’d miss. Site and Week in A4:B4 were never bold: select the whole header row 4 with Shift+Space and one Ctrl+B bolds every header at once.
  4. Ctrl+↓ from A4 lands on the total; Shift+Space, Ctrl+B. A total row is bold on every page in the pack. Jump down the site list to the Total in A11, select the whole row 11 with Shift+Space and make the total row bold.
  5. The Borders menu is Alt, H, B, then a letter: P top, O bottom, A all borders, N none. A top border on the total survives rows inserted above it; a grid has to be redrawn every time. A total takes a top border, never a grid: with row 11 still selected, add the top border with Alt, H, B, P.
  6. Fill Color is Alt, H, H, then Enter for the first tint or the arrows to another swatch. A fill marks a block of inputs so a reader can find them; it never carries meaning about the value itself. On Inputs, the cells a reader may change are B3:B6: select the block and tint it light blue with Alt, H, H, then Enter.
  7. Fonts and borders ride on the cells, and the SUM underneath doesn’t care, so the total still moves. Does it tie? Change Cedar Park’s washes in C9 to 300 and watch the Total in C11 answer, in bold above its top border.
