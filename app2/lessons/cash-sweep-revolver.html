Lesson 165: The cash sweep and the revolver

Finance and Accounting · Linking the statements · hard · Paid

The revolver draws when cash would fall below the minimum and repays when there is surplus: the draw is MAX(minimum − cash before the revolver, 0) and the repayment is MIN(surplus, opening balance), so it’s MIN and MAX again, never an IF tower. The cash flow and the balance sheet already read its rows. Build the block, and see the circle from 5.3.5 close. The key is =.

What you will do

  1. A model can’t let cash go below what the business needs to trade, and it shouldn’t let cash pile up while it pays interest. The revolver is the last line of financing: it draws when cash before it would fall below the minimum and repays when there is surplus. The revolver’s opening balance in C98:J98, cash before the revolver in C99:J99 from the CF, and minimum cash in C100:J100.
  2. The draw in C101:J101: the minimum less the cash before the revolver, floored at zero with MAX.
  3. The repayment in C102:J102, as a negative: the surplus over the minimum, capped at the opening balance with MIN.
  4. This closes the circle from 5.3.5: interest moves net income, net income moves cash, cash moves the revolver, and the revolver moves interest. Iterative calculation settles it, and Circ on Inputs switches it off. The closing balance, the average and interest in C103:J105, interest through the Circ breaker like the other tranches.
  5. The revolver’s effective rate in C106:J106: interest over the average balance, inside IFERROR.
  6. Does it tie? Watch capex per site go to $6.0m: the revolver draws in the rollout years, and cash holds at the minimum.
