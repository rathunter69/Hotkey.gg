Lesson 35: The checks row

Foundations · Present and audit · medium · Free

The Report is ready to print, but nothing on it says whether it's right. The CFO wants a line for each thing that must agree before the page goes out, and so will every buyer after. A check is a live difference between two things that must agree: it reads zero when they tie and shows the gap when they don't. Three of them under the week summary make a checks row, the first thing a reviewer looks for on any page. The key is =.

What you will do

  1. The labels are "Report revenue ties to the feed", "Sites sum to the total" and "Margin within 0-100%". A check has a name that says what it compares. Head a Checks block under the week summary: Checks in A33, bold, then the three check labels in A34:A36 as one Enter-and-↓ run.
  2. =SUM(D5:D8,D10)-Raw!J13: the sum of what the page shows, less what the feed says. Zero means the page and the feed agree. Cedar Park's typed row is left out because the feed doesn't carry it yet. In B34, take the five linked sites' revenue D5:D8 and D10 and subtract the feed's own total, pointing at Raw's J13 across sheets.
  3. A total that was typed over, or a row inserted below the block, shows up here as a non-zero, which is about as cheap as insurance gets. In B35, the six sites' washes must sum to the Total row: enter =SUM(C5:C10)-C11, and it reads 0 while the total row is honest.
  4. IF and AND arrive properly in Chapter 3; here they make a plausibility check: a margin can't be negative or over 100%, so if it is, the check fires. Sanity checks catch what tie-outs miss. In B36, a margin outside 0 to 100% is a mistake somewhere: enter =IF(AND(H11>=0,H11<=1),0,1), which reads 0 while the margin is plausible.
  5. Ctrl+1, Number, no decimals, the separator, (1,234): that's the desk number format from 1.5.1, and it matters here because a check can go negative, and then it reads (12), never -12. A checks row reads as a column of zeros, so anything else jumps out at you. Give the three checks B34:B36 the desk number format, so all three read a plain 0 and a failing one would read in parentheses.
  6. Ctrl+[ on a check shows exactly which cells it compares, which answers "what does this check check?" in one press, and F5 then Enter brings you back to where you were. A reviewer will ask what the first check compares: from B34, Ctrl+[ follows the trail to D5, the first revenue figure it reads.
  7. That's the checks row doing its job: a typed number where a link belonged, and the page says so before anyone else does. Undo puts the link back. Does it tie? Type a number over Mueller's revenue link in D6 and watch the revenue check in B34 leave zero, then Ctrl+Z.
