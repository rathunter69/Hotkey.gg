Lesson 183: Tie-outs and cross-foots

Finance and Accounting · Auditing a model · medium · Paid

A tie-out proves a figure in two places is the same figure; a cross-foot proves a block adds both ways, down the lines and across the years. Checks already ties the balance sheet, cash, debt, PP&E and revenue. Fill the three rows still marked pending, add two limit checks that count balances gone below zero, and read the flag on the Cover. The key is =.

What you will do

  1. Equity rolls like every schedule: opening, plus net income, less distributions, is closing. Written as a live difference wrapped in ROUND, it reads zero in every year the roll holds. Tie the equity roll across C12:J12: =ROUND(BS!C21+BS!C22+BS!C23-BS!C24,2), entered with Ctrl+Enter.
  2. The historical EBITDA has to agree with the P&L the buyers saw in Chapter 2, typed blue in row 48 with its source. In the projected years there is nothing to tie, so the IF reads 0 there. Tie EBITDA to the Chapter 2 P&L across C13:J13: =IF(Inputs!C$6=0,ROUND(IS!C24-C48,2),0).
  3. A cross-foot adds a block twice: every cost line across every year, and the site-cost total row across the years. If one line is left out of a total, the two sums part. Cross-foot the cost build in C11: =ROUND(SUM(Schedules!C32:J37)-SUM(Schedules!C38:J38),2).
  4. A tie-out cannot see a balance that has gone through zero: a debt tranche repaid past nothing still ties to itself. A limit check counts what should never happen, with COUNTIF and "<0", and reads zero while none does. Count debt balances below zero across C17:J17, with COUNTIF and "<0" on Schedules rows 82, 92 and 103.
  5. Do the same for closing PP&E across C18:J18, with COUNTIF on Schedules row 65.
  6. The roll-up already adds every row from the balance check down, so the new rows fold into the flag as they land. A pending note says a check is not built yet; once it is, the note goes. Clear the pending notes in K11:K13 with Clear All, then Go To Cover!C7: the flag reads OK.
  7. Does it tie? Watch FY29 PP&E on the BS typed over: the PP&E tie in H9 names the line, and the flag turns to CHECK.
