Lesson 203: Sort the set, filter the outliers, take the median

Valuation · Trading comps · hard · Paid

A set of six multiples has a shape, and one of the six is a struggling operator at 6x that would drag an average down. Sort the set to see it, decide which comp to exclude and say why in a note, and take the median (the middle value, which an outlier can’t move) plus the 25th and 75th percentiles as the range. Mean, median, low and high sit on their own rows, with a switch to include or exclude each comp. The key is MEDIAN.

What you will do

  1. Exclude a comp in the open, with a flag and a reason, never by deleting its row. The Include column holds a typed 1 or 0 for each comp, blue like any input. Flag the set in N5:N10: a 1 for each comp, then a 0 for Harbor in N9.
  2. Below the set sits a values copy of it, so the sort can’t scramble a formula. Sorted by multiple, the shape shows at once: five companies between 8.7x and 12.0x and one at 6.1x. Sort the copy by EV / EBITDA, largest first: select C35:C40, press Alt, A, S, D, and expand the selection.
  3. Two columns say why a multiple is high or low. Growth compares this LTM with the four quarters before it; net debt over EBITDA is leverage, and a net-cash comp like Summit reads negative. LTM growth in P5:P10 =J5/Q21-1, and net debt / EBITDA in Q5:Q10 =(F5-G5)/J5.
  4. Clearcoat goes on its own row under the set, linked green from the model: FY26E stands in for LTM, as the note in AA17 says. Its margin, growth and leverage then read against the set’s. Clearcoat on row 17: link I17:J17 to IS, then its margin, growth and leverage in M17, P17 and Q17.
  5. The reason for an exclusion comes from the columns, not from the multiple: a comp is out because it isn’t a peer, never because its number is inconvenient. Write why Harbor is out in AB9: its growth, its margin and its leverage against the rest of the set.
  6. MEDIAN, AVERAGE, MIN and MAX skip text in a range, so a helper column that shows the multiple for an included comp and "" for an excluded one drops the outlier out instead of counting it as zero. You’ll also see =MEDIAN(IF(include=1,multiples)) on other people’s sheets; the helper is the standard here. The helper in O5:O10: =IF(N5=1,L5,"").
  7. The median is the middle value, and the 25th and 75th percentiles from QUARTILE.INC are the low and high of the range: half the included set sits between them. Under the helper, O11:O16: MEDIAN, AVERAGE, QUARTILE.INC 1 and 3, MIN and MAX, each on O5:O10.
  8. Under margin, growth and leverage, from M11: MEDIAN, AVERAGE, MIN and MAX of M, P and Q in rows 11, 12, 15 and 16.
  9. The outlier’s pull shows when it comes back in: the mean drops by about seven tenths of a turn, the median by a quarter. That is why the range is built on the median. Flip Harbor in: type 1 in N9, read the mean in O12 and the median in O11, then type 0 again.
  10. Does it tie? Watch Prairie’s price in C10 go from $15.60 to $20.00: its multiple climbs, and the median in O11 and the quartiles answer.
