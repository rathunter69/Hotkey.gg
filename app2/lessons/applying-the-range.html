Lesson 205: Applying the range to Clearcoat

Valuation · Trading comps · medium · Paid

A range of multiples times Clearcoat’s EBITDA is a range of enterprise values: low, median, high. That’s the comps row of the football field, and it’s the first place the buyers will anchor. Build it for all three multiples, in one block that reads the statistics block. Trading multiples price minority stakes, and a control buyer pays more, which is where precedents come in. The key is *.

What you will do

  1. A multiple and the figure it multiplies cover the same period, so an LTM multiple belongs on LTM EBITDA. Clearcoat’s row uses FY26E as the proxy, flagged in AA17: this set carries no forward estimates. The block reads the statistics block, so it follows every flag. The EV / EBITDA range: C44:E44 read O13, O11 and O14, and C45:E45 =C44*$J$17 across.
  2. The same on EV per site: C46:E46 read Y13, Y11 and Y14, and C47:E47 =C46*$R$17*1000 across.
  3. And on EV per wash: C48:E48 read Z13, Z11 and Z14, and C49:E49 =C48*$S$17 across.
  4. Enterprise value belongs to everyone who funds the business; the owners get what is left after net debt (5.6.5). The same net debt comes off each range, linked green from the DCF page. Net debt in C50:E50 =-DCF!$C$43, then each equity range in rows 51 to 53: its EV plus that row.
  5. The block prints the way the book does: USD millions to one decimal from the format, and the median bold, because the median is the number the page leads with. Bold the median column, D44:D53, with Ctrl+B.
  6. Does it tie? Watch Harbor’s flag in N9 go from 0 to 1: the median falls, and the whole range in C44:E53 moves with it.
