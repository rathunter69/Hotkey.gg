Lesson 53: Borders that mean something

Formatting and presentation · The page a buyer reads · medium · Paid

undefined

What you will do

  1. A grid on every cell says nothing, because every line is the same line. No Border (Alt, H, B, N) takes every edge off the selection at once. The export left a grid over C7:E24: select it and take every border off with Alt, H, B, N.
  2. No Border means every edge, so the divider went with the grid. It is the one line that was meant, and it goes straight back. Put the divider back on D7:D24 with Alt, H, B, R.
  3. A top border says the row adds up what is above it, so every total takes one: Alt, H, B, P. F4 repeats it down the page. Give total revenue B10:E10 a top border with Alt, H, B, P, then B20:E20, B22:E22 and B24:E24 with F4.
  4. The double bottom marks the one final answer on the page, and EBITDA is it. On Ctrl+1’s Border tab, ↓ reaches the line styles and ↑ from None lands on the double. Give EBITDA B24:E24 a double bottom from Ctrl+1’s Border tab.
  5. Turn the gridlines off with Alt, W, V, G, so the borders are the only lines on the page.
  6. Does it tie? Watch C13 change to -4500, and the bordered totals in C20, C22 and C24 answer.
