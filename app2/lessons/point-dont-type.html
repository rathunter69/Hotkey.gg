Lesson 27: Point, don’t type

Foundations · Formulas · medium · Free

undefined

What you will do

  1. Type =, press ← twice and D5 writes itself into the formula, type the minus, press ← once for E5, Enter. While a formula is open, each arrow key points at a cell and writes its reference; the cell you’re pointing at is outlined in color. If the cell shows the formula’s text instead of a result, the = is missing, or has a space or an apostrophe in front of it. Domain’s gross profit is revenue less wash cost: build =D5-E5 in F5 by pointing at the two cells with the arrow keys, not typing them.
  2. Ctrl and an arrow works while pointing too, jumping the pointer to the edge the way it jumps the cursor, so a long row costs you no counting. Domain’s average ticket is revenue divided by washes: build =D5/C5 in G5 by pointing, jumping the pointer across the row with Ctrl+←.
  3. Third formula, same move. Margin is what’s left of a dollar of revenue after the wash costs, a buyer’s first question about any site. Domain’s margin is gross profit divided by revenue: build =F5/D5 in H5 the same way.
  4. F2 on a formula colors each reference and outlines the cell it points at, the fastest audit there is. Read a formula before you fill it; a wrong one filled down is six wrong ones. Read one back before you trust it: open F5 with F2 so its inputs D5 and E5 light up in color on the sheet, then leave it unchanged with Esc.
  5. Ctrl+D fills the top row’s formulas down the selection, and each reference shifts a row as it goes: D5-E5 becomes D6-E6. That’s a relative reference, and it’s why one formula per row is the rule. Five sites still have no calculated lines: select F5:H10 with Domain’s three formulas at the top and fill them down with Ctrl+D.
  6. A fill carries formats as well as formulas, so the $ from the first row went with it. Copy E6 and Paste Special Formats (Ctrl+Alt+V, T) onto F6:F10 to put the body back in the desk number format; the $ stays on F5 and the total row. Next time, copy the formulas and Paste Special Formulas (Ctrl+Alt+V, F, from 1.3.4), and the formats underneath are never touched. The fill carried Domain’s $ sign down the gross-profit column: put F6:F10, and only those, back in the desk number format.
  7. F2 opens the cell in Edit mode, where the arrows move the caret inside the formula. Backspace the 1.5 out and press F2 again (the status bar, bottom left, goes from Edit to Enter), and now ↑ ten times points at B4, the way the arrows point in a new formula. One input, one cell, every formula references it: when the chemical price changes, B4 changes and B14 follows. On Inputs, B14 still has the cost typed inside it, =B6*1.5: change it to =B6*B4, so it references the input cell.
  8. A name works anywhere an address does, and reads like English. Use names for a handful of key inputs (the price, the rate, the toggle), never for everything; Chapter 4 sets the rules. Same formula, other way to say it: change B14 to =B6*CostPerWash, the name you gave B4 in 1.3.5.
  9. The three lines are live now. Change one input and every formula that reads it moves. The page is starting to behave like a model. Does it tie? Change Domain’s washes in C5 to 300 and watch its average ticket in G5 answer.
