Lesson 27: SUM family and AutoSum

Foundations · Formulas · medium · Free

undefined

What you will do

  1. AutoSum, Alt+=, on one empty cell proposes a SUM over the numbers above; over a whole block it writes every column’s SUM into the row below in one press. Select the six sites’ figures C5:F10 and press Alt+= once: every column’s SUM lands in the total row, Gross profit’s F11 included.
  2. The total row needs its average price: G11 is total revenue over total kWh, D11 divided by C11, pointed the way the site rows were.
  3. The total row needs its margin: H11 is total gross profit over total revenue, F11 divided by D11, pointed the same way.
  4. Start a Week summary block under the report: Week summary in A26, bold, then the five line labels A27:A31 as one Enter run.
  5. SUM, AVERAGE, MAX, MIN and COUNT each take one range, =AVERAGE(C5:C10), and a blank inside the range is left out, not counted as zero. Type the first three lines: B27 =AVERAGE(C5:C10) for the average site, B28 =MAX(C5:C10) for the best, B29 =MIN(C5:C10) for the lowest.
  6. The three kWh lines B27:B29 read as figures: thousands separators and no decimals, the same comma format the report above uses.
  7. COUNT counts the numbers in a range and COUNTA counts every non-empty cell, text included, so COUNT checks the figures and COUNTA checks the names. Count the figures and the names: B30 =COUNT(C5:C10) counts the kWh figures, B31 =COUNTA(A5:A10) counts the site names.
  8. Does it tie? Watch Riverside’s kWh in C7 clear: the average in B27 and the count in B30 answer, and Sites listed in B31 does not.
