Lesson 164: Discounting and the mid-year convention

Finance and Accounting · DCF · hard · Paid

A discount factor is 1 over (1 + WACC) to the t, and the question is what t is. Cash arrives through the year, not on December 31, so the mid-year convention discounts year 1 at 0.5, year 2 at 1.5, and so on. The present values, summed, plus the discounted terminal value, are enterprise value; less net debt is equity value. Build it, and read what the convention is worth. The key is ^.

What you will do

  1. The period counter is the year count from Inputs, less a half when the mid-year switch is on, and zero in the historical years, so only the forecast is discounted. Period t across C20:J20: =IF(Inputs!C$6=1,Inputs!C$8-IF(Inputs!$C$97=1,0.5,0),0), entered with Ctrl+Enter.
  2. The caret raises to a power: 1/(1+WACC)^t is what a dollar t years out is worth today. The discount factor across C21:J21: =IF(Inputs!C$6=1,1/(1+WACC)^C20,0).
  3. Present values across C22:J22: =C16*C21, then their sum in C23: =SUM(C22:J22).
  4. An exit is a sale at the end of FY31, so it takes the end-year factor whatever the switch says; a perpetuity’s cash keeps arriving through the year, so it takes year 5’s own factor. Discount factors for the terminal values: C36 =J21, and C37 =1/(1+WACC)^Inputs!$J$8.
  5. Discount each terminal value: C38 =C27*C36, and C39 =C28*C37.
  6. Enterprise value is the discounted forecast plus the discounted terminal value, built once per method so each sensitivity table can read its own. The switch picks which one goes on. Enterprise value: C40 =C23+C38, C41 =C23+C39, and C42 =CHOOSE(C32,C40,C41).
  7. Picture a house: enterprise value is what the house is worth, net debt the mortgage, and equity the owner’s stake. Net debt is taken at the valuation date, the FY26 year end. Net debt in C43: =Schedules!$E$111, and equity value in C44: =C42-C43.
  8. How much of the value rests on the terminal value is the first thing a reviewer reads. The multiples get read against the comps: FY27’s is lower while EBITDA grows. The memos: C45 =CHOOSE(C32,C38,C39)/C42, C46 =C23/C42, and E47:F47 =$C$42/E8.
  9. NPV discounts each flow a whole period out, which is the end-year case, so a SUMPRODUCT at end-year periods must match it exactly. The cross-check: C24 =SUMPRODUCT($F$16:$J$16,1/(1+WACC)^Inputs!$F$8:$J$8), then on Checks C27: =ROUND(DCF!C24-NPV(WACC,DCF!F16:J16),2).
  10. Does it tie? Watch the mid-year switch on Inputs go off: the present values in C23 fall about 5%, and the exit value stays put.
