Lesson 85: Serial numbers: DATE, YEAR, MONTH, DAY

Formulas and functions · Dates · medium · Paid

undefined

What you will do

  1. Ctrl+Shift+~ is the General format: it strips the date costume and shows the serial underneath. Ctrl+Z puts the costume back. Go to the as-of date on Sites, I5, show its serial with Ctrl+Shift+~, read 46,280, then put the date back with Ctrl+Z.
  2. Age in days: J5 =$I$5-E5, anchored on the one as-of date, filled down to J10.
  3. Age in years: K5 =J5/365.25, a quarter day a year for the leap years, filled down to K10.
  4. YEAR, MONTH and DAY each take one part of a date: YEAR(E5) is 2019 for a site opened on 3/15/2019. Take the opening date apart in L5:N5 as a Tab run, =YEAR(E5), =MONTH(E5) and =DAY(E5), then fill the three down to row 10.
  5. A vintage is the year a site opened. A buyer reads sites by vintage because the 2019 sites have had seven years to ramp and the 2026 one has had a week. Put YEAR to work: the vintage in P5 is =YEAR(E5), filled down to P10, so the page can be read by the year each site opened.
  6. DATE(year, month, day) builds a date from three numbers, and it rolls over: DATE(2026,13,1) is January 1, 2027. Build Domain’s month opened in O5 from the parts: =DATE(L5,M5,1) is the first of its opening month, 3/1/2019.
  7. Fill it down O5:O10 with Ctrl+D: every site gets the first of its opening month, the date a count by month groups on.
  8. The ages on Summary were typed in 3.1.3 for want of this lesson. Now they link to Sites, so a new as-of date moves every flag that reads them. Replace the typed ages on Summary: select L5:L10, type =Sites!K5, press Ctrl+Enter and color them green as links with Alt H F C.
  9. Does it tie? Watch the as-of date in Sites!I5 move to 12/31/2026 and every age in J and K move with it.
