Lesson 13: Fix it in place

Foundations · Enter, edit, copy and fill · medium · Free

The feed is complete but not clean: two site names are misspelled, one wash count was typed as text, and one day’s revenue is ten times its neighbors. Analysts find these by flicking through cells (arrow to a cell, F2 to see exactly what is in it, Esc to leave it untouched) and fix them in place with F2, Home, End and the arrows, changing only what is wrong. Ctrl+Z undoes a change and Ctrl+Y puts it back; F4 repeats a fix on the next cell. The key is F2.

What you will do

  1. Move to Raw and flick down the Site column from B10: ↓, F2, Esc, until you find Muller in B14.
  2. In Edit mode, Home and End send the insertion point to either end of the entry and ← → step it one character; typing inserts where the caret is, and Enter keeps the change. Open B14 with F2, move the insertion point to just after Mu, and insert the e so it reads Mueller.
  3. Keep flicking: Riverside lost its e in one row further down, B27, so read each before you edit it, then add the e.
  4. A figure sitting on the left is text, not a number, and a total that reads it will skip it. Backspace deletes the character before the insertion point, so the trailing space goes and 240 snaps to the right. C33 shows 240 sitting on the left, which means text with a trailing space: open it, delete the space and commit it as a number.
  5. Typing on a cell replaces its whole entry: no F2 when nothing in the old entry is worth keeping. Sanity-check the magnitude; one site does not do $81,500 in a day. E41 reads 81500 for one day of South Lamar revenue when washes times ticket says 3,150: type 3150 over it.
  6. Ctrl+Z undoes the last change and Ctrl+Y puts it back, dozens of steps deep, so a fix can be tried, read and kept, or shown to someone who asks. The CFO asks what was there before: Ctrl+Z, read 81500, then Ctrl+Y to put the fix back.
  7. Ctrl+F is for when you know the text but not the cell: type part of it, Enter jumps to the next match, Esc closes the box and leaves you there. One typo you cannot see from here, Airprot somewhere in sixty rows: Ctrl+F finds it, then type Airport over it.
  8. F4 repeats your last action on the cell you are on now: a format, a border, a width, an insert. Fix it once, F4 the rest. It does not repeat typing; for that there is Ctrl+Enter. Whoever flagged the errors filled E41, C33 and B14 yellow: clear the fill on E41 with Alt, H, H, N, then F4 on C33 and F4 on B14.
  9. Does it tie? Change C33, a Riverside wash count, to 260 and watch Riverside’s total in I10 answer.
