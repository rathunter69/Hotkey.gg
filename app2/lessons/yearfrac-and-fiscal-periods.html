Lesson 60: YEARFRAC and fiscal periods

Formulas and functions · Dates · medium · Paid

undefined

What you will do

  1. YEARFRAC(start, end) is the years between two dates. With no basis it counts 30/360, a 30-day month and a 360-day year, the bond convention; a basis of 1 counts actual days, the one a lease uses. On Sites, select the ages K5:K10, type =YEARFRAC(E5,$I$5) and press Ctrl+Enter to replace the /365.25.
  2. A stub is a part year. The desk’s main use of YEARFRAC is the annual figure times YEARFRAC(start, period end): what a site opened in September can hold in its opening year. Cedar Park opened on 9/8/2026, so 2026 is a stub year: in C15, =YEARFRAC(E10,DATE(2026,12,31)) reads 0.31 of a year.
  3. A fiscal year is named for the calendar year it ends in. The buyer’s ends June 30, so a date from July on belongs to the next year: September 2026 is FY2027. On Transactions, the buyer’s fiscal year in L5 is =IF(MONTH(A5)>=7,YEAR(A5)+1,YEAR(A5)); Tab on.
  4. RIGHT(L5,2) keeps the last two characters, 27 from 2027, so the label reads FY27; the text functions come in full in 3.4. The half in M5 is =IF(MONTH(A5)>=7,"H1","H2") and the label in N5 is ="FY"&RIGHT(L5,2)&" "&M5, a Tab run.
  5. The 7 inside these formulas is the buyer’s first fiscal month. In a model it lives in one blue input cell and every formula points at it, so a buyer with a March year end is one edit away. Fill the three to the last row: Go To L94, widen to N, Ctrl+Shift+↑ up to row 5 and Ctrl+D.
  6. Does it tie? Watch the first wash move to 6/30/2026, the buyer’s year end: its label in N5 flips to FY26 H2.
