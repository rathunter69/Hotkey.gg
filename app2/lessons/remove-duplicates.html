Lesson 126: Remove Duplicates and the unique site list

Data and Lookups · Lists and tables · medium · Paid

A clean list of sites is the spine of every summary, and the export has each site fifteen times. Remove Duplicates (Alt, A, M) keeps the first of each and deletes the rest, so it runs on a copy of the column, never on the export. Then COUNTIF against the original proves the list is complete, and the misspelled code the export carries shows up as a seventh site. The key is Alt A M.

What you will do

  1. Copy the export’s Site column, Export!B4:B94, and paste it on Lists at N4.
  2. Remove Duplicates (Alt, A, M) keeps the first row of each value and deletes the rest, then tells you how many went. The ticked columns define a duplicate: tick two and a row goes only when the pair repeats. With the pasted column selected, Alt, A, M and Enter: read the seven codes left, one of them AUS-DMO.
  3. On Export, Ctrl+F finds AUS-DMO; press Esc and type AUS-DOM over it.
  4. Back on Lists, the typo no longer exists in the export: clear AUS-DMO from the list, leaving six codes.
  5. Beside each code, O5:O10, count its rows on the export: =COUNTIF(Export!$B$5:$B$94,N5) with Ctrl+Enter.
  6. Total the counts in O11 with Alt+=, and in C26 check them against the export: =O11-COUNTA(Export!$B$5:$B$94).
  7. Does it tie? Watch a new code typed into the export leave the COUNTIF proof one short, and the check in C26 say so.
