Lesson 61: A navigation column for a long sheet

Formatting and presentation · Alignment and structure · medium · Paid

Monthly detail runs to seventy-five rows once the four clusters and the company block are in, and a reader shouldn’t scroll to find the EBITDA line. A navigation column is a short list at the top of the sheet: each entry a hyperlink to a named block, so following it lands you there, and Ctrl+G with the name does the same. Name the blocks, list them, link them. The key is Ctrl+K.

What you will do

  1. A defined name labels a range, and Go To accepts the name wherever it accepts an address. Name the blocks a reader jumps to and nothing else. On Monthly detail, name the company’s total revenue B66:O66 Rev with Define Name, Alt, M, M, D.
  2. Name the company’s total site costs on the row under it, B67:O67, SiteCosts.
  3. Name the company’s EBITDA row B70:O70 EBITDA.
  4. The navigation column sits at the top of the sheet, beside the frozen header, where a reader starts. Each entry says the block in words; the name behind it does the jumping. Type the navigation column: Go to in Q4 in bold, then Revenue, Site costs and EBITDA in Q5:Q7.
  5. Ctrl+K opens Insert Hyperlink. Place in This Document (Alt+A) lists the sheets and then the defined names; Alt+C reaches the list, ↓ walks it, and Enter links the cell. The cell keeps its words and takes the hyperlink look. Link Revenue in Q5 to its block: Ctrl+K, Place in This Document with Alt+A, Alt+C to the list, ↓ twice to Rev, Enter.
  6. Link Site costs in Q6 to SiteCosts and EBITDA in Q7 to EBITDA the same way.
  7. Jump to the EBITDA line by name: Ctrl+G, EBITDA, Enter.
  8. Come back to the top with Ctrl+Home, which lands on C5 under the frozen panes.
  9. Does it tie? Watch the EBITDA link followed from Q7, head office in C69 change to -600, and the company’s EBITDA in C70 answer.
