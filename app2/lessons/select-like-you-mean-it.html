Lesson 8: Select like you mean it

Foundations · Move and select · easy · Free

undefined

What you will do

  1. Shift and an arrow grows a selection a cell at a time, and a command then lands on every cell in it. Numbers align right and text aligns left, so a header over a number column reads best right-aligned: Alt, H, A, then L, C or R. On Raw, select the figure headers C1:F1 a cell at a time with Shift+→ and right-align them over their numbers with Alt, H, A, R.
  2. Ctrl+Shift and an arrow grows the selection to the edge of the data: sixty cells in one press, and the Name Box reads E1:E60. Glance at the status bar before you use any column, because it’s how you catch a text figure or a missing day before it costs you. Select Revenue from its header E1 to the last figure with one Ctrl+Shift+↓, then read its sum in the status bar.
  3. Shift+Space selects the entire row of the active cell, edge to edge, and any arrow on its own collapses a selection back to one cell. Row Height (Alt, H, O, H) is in points: type it and Enter. AutoFit Row Height (Alt, H, O, A) sizes a row back to what its text needs. Give the header row some air: select row 1 with Shift+Space and set its height to 20 with Alt, H, O, H.
  4. Ctrl+Space selects the entire column, and AutoFit Column Width (Alt, H, O, I) sizes it to its longest entry. Row and column selections are how you insert, delete, hide and resize, all of module 1.4. On a Mac, ⌃Space may be taken by the system; ⌘⇧↑ then ⌘⇧↓ from the top of a column does the job. The site names in column B are cut off: select the column with Ctrl+Space and AutoFit it with Alt, H, O, I.
  5. Ctrl+A selects the current region: the block of data around the active cell, bounded by blank rows and columns, and a second Ctrl+A takes the whole sheet. Count is how many cells hold something, so a feed that comes up short is a figure somebody still owes you. You’ll fill those nine in module 1.3. Select the whole feed with Ctrl+A and read Count in the status bar: 357 of its 366 cells are filled, so nine are blank.
  6. Ctrl+Shift+End selects from the active cell to the last used cell on the sheet, gaps included, where Ctrl+A stops at the first blank row. The Name Box tells you how far a sheet really runs before you send it or print it. Go home with Ctrl+Home and take everything in use with Ctrl+Shift+End: A1:N67, the feed, its notes and the two blocks beside it.
