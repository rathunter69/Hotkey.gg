Lesson 156: Accrual and cash: why profit isn’t cash

Finance and Accounting · The three statements · medium · Paid

A member pays $30 on the 1st for washes they’ll take all month, so on the 1st the cash is in the bank and none of the revenue is earned; the unearned part is deferred revenue, a liability, because the company owes the member washes. The chemical supplier is paid in thirty days, so September’s chemicals are a cost in September and cash in October: a payable. Card revenue settles in three days: a receivable. Profit and cash differ by timing gaps like these, so build them for Domain’s month. The key is =.

What you will do

  1. Accrual accounting books revenue when it is earned and a cost when it is incurred, whenever the cash moves. A member’s fee arrives on the 1st, so the cash is in and the revenue is still owed to the member as washes. Membership cash in C55: members times the fee paid on the 1st, =C22*C23, which reads $45,000.
  2. The fee is earned a thirtieth a day, so the unearned part shrinks as the month runs: half at the 15th, none at the 30th. That unearned balance is deferred revenue, a liability on the balance sheet. Deferred revenue at September 15 in C56, =C55*(C25-15)/C25, and at September 30 in C57, =C55*(C25-C25)/C25.
  3. One member who paid on the 20th: in C58, =C23*(C25-10)/C25, two thirds of the fee still unearned on the 30th.
  4. September’s chemicals are a cost in September and cash in October, so at the month end the whole bill is a payable. Chemicals count as used on delivery, so there is no inventory line. A year of insurance paid ahead is a prepaid asset, the mirror of deferred revenue, and wages worked but not yet paid are an accrued liability, the payable’s twin; neither has a row here. Payables at September 30 in C59: the month’s chemicals, unpaid until October, =-C34.
  5. Card sales land in the bank three days later, so the last three days of the month are revenue booked and cash not yet in: a receivable, an asset. Receivables at September 30 in C60: three days of card revenue, =C33/C25*C24.
  6. Cash from operations starts at net income, adds back depreciation because no cash left for it, then adjusts for the gaps: a rise in a liability is cash kept, a rise in an asset is cash not yet in. Best practice: it is the change in a balance that moves cash, never the balance itself. Cash from operations by hand in C61: =C52-C47+(C59-C27)-(C60-C28)+C57, net income adjusted for every gap.
  7. Does it tie? Watch August’s unpaid bill in C27 go to nil: September pays nothing for it, and cash from operations rises by $10,800.
