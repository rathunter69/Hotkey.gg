Lesson 21: Numbers a banker can read

Foundations · Format · medium · Free

undefined

What you will do

  1. A number format changes how a value shows, not the value itself: Ctrl+1 opens Format Cells, whose categories answer to their first letter, N Number, C Currency, P Percentage. Select the kWh sold figures C5:C11, Total included, and give them thousands separators with no decimals: Ctrl+1, then N.
  2. Give Revenue, Energy cost and Gross profit D5:F11 comma style in one press, Ctrl+Shift+1, then Alt H 9 twice: negatives read in parentheses.
  3. The $ goes on a money column’s first and total rows only: make D5:F5, D11:F11 and Prior week’s I5 currency with no decimals, Ctrl+1 then C.
  4. Prior week rev I6:I10 gets thousands separators with no decimals, Cedar Park’s empty I9 included, while I5 keeps the column’s $.
  5. Avg price ($/kWh) shows cents: select G5:G11 from the bottom, Ctrl+Shift+↑ then Shift+↓ off the header, and give it currency, Ctrl+Shift+4.
  6. Margin % H5:H11 reads as a percentage to one decimal: select it the same way, Ctrl+Shift+5 for percent, then Alt H 0 for the decimal.
  7. The daily table B17:G21 will hold thirty kWh figures: format the whole block at once, comma style with Ctrl+Shift+1, then Alt H 9 twice.
  8. On Inputs, the wholesale energy price B4 is quoted to the tenth of a cent: currency with Ctrl+Shift+4, then Alt H 0 for the third decimal.
  9. Does it tie? Watch Cedar Park’s kWh in C9 change to 3,000 and the Total in C11 answer, thousands separator and all.
