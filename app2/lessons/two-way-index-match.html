Lesson 118: Two-way INDEX/MATCH: any site, any week

Data and lookups · Lookups · medium · Paid

A two-way lookup finds a row and a column: the washes for any site in any week, from the cube on Summary. INDEX takes a whole block and two positions, one MATCH for the site down the side and one for the week across the top, and one cell answers any pair. The cube is pasted values for now; 4.3.1 builds it live from the export. Wire the answer to two typed inputs so a buyer can ask any site, any week. The key is INDEX.

What you will do

  1. Select the washes in the cube on Summary, C15:E20: sites run down B15:B20, weeks across C14:E14.
  2. Type the question into the blue inputs: AUS-AIR in C34 and Week of 21-Sep in C35.
  3. A two-way lookup needs two positions. MATCH down the codes in B15:B20 says which row of the cube the site is on, and MATCH across the week labels in C14:E14 says which column. Row position in C36: =MATCH(C34,$B$15:$B$20,0) finds the site down the side.
  4. Column position in C37: =MATCH(C35,$C$14:$E$14,0) finds the week across the top.
  5. INDEX(block, row, column) returns the cell where a row and a column of the block cross, so the two positions read one figure out of eighteen. In C38, =INDEX($C$15:$E$20,C36,C37) reads the washes where the two positions cross.
  6. The helper cells show the working; the one-cell version is what goes in a model, because it can’t lose a helper. Best practice: keep the inputs in their own blue cells and never type them inside the formula. Collapse it into one cell: put both MATCHes inside the INDEX in C38.
  7. Does it tie? Watch the inputs change to AUS-MUE and Week of 28-Sep and C38 move with them.
