Lesson 42: Sign convention: costs negative, stated once

Formatting and presentation · Number formats · medium · Paid

The export shows every cost as a positive, so a reader has to know which lines to subtract. On a page we show costs as negatives, in parentheses, and say so once at the top: then the page adds down and nobody guesses the signs. Chemicals and water are the cost of a wash; the crew, rent, utilities, maintenance, card fees and marketing are the site costs a site pays whether or not a car comes through. Paste Special Multiply flips a whole block in one paste. The key is Ctrl+Alt+V.

What you will do

  1. Type -1 in the spare cell G19, beside the last cost line, then copy it with Ctrl+C.
  2. Paste Special Multiply, Ctrl+Alt+V then V and M, multiplies every selected cell by the copied value, so one -1 flips a whole block. Select the cost lines C13:E19, stopping above the total in row 20, and Paste Special Multiply with Ctrl+Alt+V, V, M, Enter.
  3. Head office in C23:E23 is a cost too: select the line and multiply it by the same -1.
  4. Site contribution still subtracts: type =C10+C20 in C22, then select C22:E22 and fill it right with Ctrl+R.
  5. EBITDA in C24 becomes =C22+C23 the same way, filled right across C24:E24 with Ctrl+R.
  6. The -1 in G19 has done its job: go back to it and clear it with Delete.
  7. State the convention once: in A2 type USD thousands unless stated; costs shown as negatives, then make it italic.
  8. Does it tie? Watch rent in C15 typed as (4500), parentheses and all, and site contribution C22 and EBITDA C24 fall.
