Lesson 215: The returns bridge

Valuation · LBO · medium · Paid

A return comes from three places, and a sponsor wants to see which: EBITDA growth (the rollout), multiple expansion (paying 11.7x and selling at 11.0x is a loss on this one), and debt paydown (the sweep). The bridge splits the equity gain into the three, takes off the fees paid on the way in, and the four lines sum to the gain. Build it, and read where this return really comes from. The key is =.

What you will do

  1. The equity gain in C122: equity at the exit less equity at entry, =C104-C105.
  2. A return comes from three places, and a sponsor wants to see which. Growth is the rollout: every dollar of EBITDA added is worth the multiple paid for it. The EBITDA growth effect in C123: FY31 adjusted EBITDA less FY26E EBITDA, times the entry multiple.
  3. Paying 11.7x and selling at 11.0x loses the difference on every dollar of FY31 EBITDA, so on this deal the multiple works against the sponsor. The multiple effect in C124: the exit multiple less the entry multiple, times FY31 adjusted EBITDA.
  4. The debt paydown effect in C125: net debt at closing less net debt at FY31.
  5. Entry equity paid the fees, and nothing at the exit returns them, so without this line the three effects overshoot the gain by exactly the fees. The fees in C126, as a negative: the 2% paid at entry that no buyer pays back.
  6. The four effects summed in C127, and the check against the gain in C128, reading zero.
  7. Each effect as a share of the gain in C129:C132.
  8. Does it tie? Watch the exit multiple in C17 set to the entry multiple: C124 reads zero and the check still holds.
