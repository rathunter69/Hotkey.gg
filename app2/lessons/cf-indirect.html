Lesson 163: The cash flow statement, indirect

Finance and Accounting · Linking the statements · medium · Paid

The cash flow statement at model scale: net income from the IS, depreciation added back, the working-capital changes with their signs, capex as a negative, the debt draws and repayments, distributions and the revolver. Then the net change, opening cash from last year’s closing, and closing cash, the line the balance sheet will read. The key is =.

What you will do

  1. The indirect cash flow starts from net income and corrects it back to cash: depreciation cost nothing in cash this year, so it goes back in, and the working-capital changes come straight off the schedule with their signs. Every line is a link to the IS or a schedule. Operations in C6:J11: net income, depreciation added back, the three working-capital changes and the subtotal.
  2. Investing in C14:J15: capex from the PP&E schedule as a negative, and the subtotal.
  3. The debt lines in C18:J21: the term loan drawn and repaid, the delayed-draw loan drawn and repaid.
  4. A line with nothing in it yet still gets its link, never a typed zero: the revolver and distributions read nil today, and a typed zero would drop the cash the day either one moves. Distributions in C22:J22, the revolver in C24:J24 from its empty rows on Schedules, and financing in C25:J25.
  5. Cash before the revolver in C23:J23, the net change in C28:J28, then opening and closing cash in C29:J30.
  6. Does it tie? Watch a 1,000 revolver draw go into FY27 on the debt schedule, and closing cash rise by 1,000.
