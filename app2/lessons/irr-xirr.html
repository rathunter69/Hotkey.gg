Lesson 54: IRR and XIRR

Formulas · Time value of money · medium · Paid

undefined

What you will do

  1. IRR(flows) finds the rate at which the flows discount to zero, so year 0 goes inside this time. It returns #NUM! when every flow has the same sign, or when its search fails from its 10% starting guess; an optional second argument, guess, gives it a new place to start. On Loans, in C36 find the site’s own return with =IRR(C29:H29), year 0 included, and read it against the 10% discount rate.
  2. Prove it: point the discount rate at the IRR with =C36 in C30, and the NPV in C34 reads zero.
  3. Put the 10% discount rate back in C30 with Ctrl+Z.
  4. XIRR(flows, dates) finds the same rate on the actual dates, by the days between them, the way XNPV discounts. On flows a year apart it lands close to IRR. In C37 build the dated return, =XIRR(C29:H29,C26:H26).
  5. Put XIRR to work: the sale slips a year, so type 10/1/2032 over the year 5 date in H26 and watch C37 fall while C36 stays.
  6. Payback reads the cash added up year by year: year 0 is its own flow, and every later year is the last running total plus that year’s flow. The year the running total turns positive is the year the build is paid back. Put the date back with Ctrl+Z, then run cumulative cash along row 38: =C29 in C38, =C38+D29 in D38, filled right to H38.
  7. In the year the total crosses zero, the part of the year it took is what was still owed at the start over that year’s flow. =IF(AND(C38<0,D38>=0),-C38/D29,0) gives that fraction in the crossing year and zero everywhere else. In C39 write =IF(AND(C38<0,D38>=0),-C38/D29,0) for the fraction of the crossing year, and fill it right to G39.
  8. In C40 the payback is the years still negative plus the fraction: =COUNTIF(C38:H38,"<0")+SUM(C39:G39).
  9. Does it tie? Watch the sale value in H28 halve and the IRR, the NPV and the payback all answer.
