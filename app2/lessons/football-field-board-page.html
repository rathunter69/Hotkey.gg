Lesson 204: The football-field table and the one-page summary for the board

Valuation · The bids and the waterfall · hard · Paid

The football field puts every valuation range on one line each: comps, precedents, the DCF, the LBO ceiling and the three bids, low, mid and high, as a table, so the board sees in one look where the bids sit against every method. Under it go the waterfall for the recommended bid and one line of recommendation. Every figure on the page is a link to the sheet that built it, a Ctrl+PgDn away. The key is Ctrl+PgDn.

What you will do

  1. A football field is every method’s range on its own line, low, mid and high, all in enterprise value. Each line links the range block of the page that built it, so the board’s page never holds a typed figure. On Summary, link the comps range to C9:E9 and the precedents range to row 10.
  2. The DCF’s range is its exit-multiple sensitivity grid, lowest to highest, with the page’s enterprise value as the mid. The LBO line is what the sponsor can pay at three hurdles: a ceiling, not a valuation. Row 11: the DCF grid’s MIN, enterprise value and MAX; row 12: the LBO’s top prices from LBO row 146.
  3. Link each bid’s expected, priced and headline value from Bids into rows 13 to 15, low to high.
  4. One ratio says where each line sits: its mid over the DCF’s mid. A bid at 95% of the DCF is a bid below what the cash flows say the business is worth. Show where each line sits in F9:F15: =D9/$D$11, entered with Ctrl+Enter.
  5. The waterfall shows one bid, the one recommended. MATCH finds that bid’s column on Bids from its letter, so changing one typed letter changes the whole block. Type A in C18 for the recommended bid, then find its column in C19: =MATCH("Bid "&C18,Bids!$C$4:$E$4,0).
  6. Pull the recommended bid’s waterfall into C20:C29 with INDEX on C19, starting =INDEX(Bids!$C$30:$E$30,$C$19).
  7. The recommendation is the one place on the page a person wrote, so it is typed, in blue, in one sentence. Everything above it is a link the board can follow. Type the recommendation in C32, one sentence: bid A, the lowest headline, has the highest expected value and is all cash at close.
  8. The page has the anatomy every page in the pack has: a title from Inputs, a units line, the model’s checks flag from the Cover, a source line under the table, and no gridlines on a page someone reads. Title A1 from Inputs, units in A2, the Cover’s flag and the valuation date in C5:C6, the source in B33, gridlines off.
  9. Fill the checks block in C36:C39: sources equal uses from Checks, the Cover’s flag, the ranges in order, the owners’ lines.
  10. Does it tie? Watch bid B’s earnout odds on Bids go to 90%: bid B’s line on the football field, row 14, answers on the board’s page.
