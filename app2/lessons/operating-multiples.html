Lesson 204: Operating multiples: EV per site, EV per wash

Valuation · Trading comps · medium · Paid

EBITDA can be dressed up; a site can’t. Car-wash buyers read enterprise value per site and per wash alongside EV / EBITDA, because a site is a unit anyone can count and a wash is what the site actually sells. Build both across the set, take their medians, and read where Clearcoat’s forty sites and 3.6m washes would sit. The key is /.

What you will do

  1. Sites and washes now sit beside each comp in R and S, typed and sourced, with whether it owns or rents its land. A comp that rents pays rent above the EBITDA line, so for the same washes it shows less EBITDA than one that owns; adding the rent back (EBITDAR) compares them evenly. EBITDA before rent in V5:V10: =J5+U5, so a comp that rents compares with one that owns.
  2. EV per site is in millions of dollars, so the thousands divide by a thousand more; EV per wash is thousands over thousands, already in dollars. EV per site in W5:W10 =H5/R5/1000, and EV per wash in X5:X10 =H5/S5.
  3. Each operating multiple gets its own helper column, read off the same Include flags, so Harbor drops out of these statistics too. The helpers: Y5:Y10 =IF(N5=1,W5,""), and Z5:Z10 =IF(N5=1,X5,"").
  4. The statistics on both helpers, Y11:Z16: median, mean, the two quartiles, minimum and maximum, one row at a time across Y and Z.
  5. Clearcoat’s own counts come from the model: forty sites and 3.6m washes in FY26E on Schedules, and its rent from IS, turned positive to add back. Clearcoat rents its land, so its EBITDAR sits well above its EBITDA. Clearcoat’s row: R17:S17 from Schedules, Rents in T17, its rent in U17 =-IS!$E$14, and EBITDAR in V17.
  6. Does it tie? Watch Pinnacle’s site count go from 400 to 500: its EV per site falls, and the median in Y11 moves with it.
