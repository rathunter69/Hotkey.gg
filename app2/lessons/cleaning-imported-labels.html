Lesson 71: Cleaning imported labels: TRIM, PROPER, SUBSTITUTE

Formatting and presentation · Dates and text for presentation · medium · Paid

Monthly detail’s labels came in the way exports do: capitals, double spaces, an underscore where a space belongs. Three functions clean them without retyping: TRIM strips stray spaces, PROPER capitalizes each word, SUBSTITUTE swaps one piece of text for another. Build the clean label beside the dirty one, then paste values over the original. The key is =.

What you will do

  1. Read it from the inside out: SUBSTITUTE swaps each _ for a space, TRIM strips the doubled and trailing spaces, and PROPER capitalizes each word. Column R is free, past the navigation column. In Monthly detail!R5 enter =PROPER(TRIM(SUBSTITUTE(B5,"_"," "))) and read Austin.
  2. Select R5:R69 with Go To and fill the formula down with Ctrl+D, to the head office line.
  3. R8 still reads Other Revenue (1): the marker belongs in a footnote, not in the label. A second SUBSTITUTE swaps "(1)" for nothing, and Ctrl+Enter writes the formula into every selected cell at once. With R5:R69 still selected, type =PROPER(TRIM(SUBSTITUTE(SUBSTITUTE(B5,"_"," "),"(1)",""))) and press Ctrl+Enter.
  4. Paste Special Values writes the words and drops the formulas, so the labels stand on their own once the helper goes. Stop at row 69: PROPER would write EBITDA in B70 as Ebitda. Copy R5:R69 and paste it as values over the labels at B5 with Ctrl+Alt+V, V.
  5. A formula that returns nothing pastes as empty text, and Excel counts empty text as content: the four gaps between the blocks now hold it. Delete makes them truly empty again. Clear the gap rows the paste filled with empty text, B19, B34, B49 and B64, with Delete.
  6. Clear the helper column R5:R69 with Delete, now that the labels stand on their own.
  7. Does it tie? Nothing here moved a number: watch Austin’s chemicals in C10 change to 900 and the total in C17 answer.
