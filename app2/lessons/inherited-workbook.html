Lesson 2: The workbook management sent

Foundations · Open and set up · easy · Free

undefined

What you will do

  1. The arrow keys move the active cell one cell per press — feel how long sixty rows takes. Round one, the slow way: walk down the dates with ↓ until you reach the last one, A61.
  2. Ctrl+Home returns to A1 and Ctrl+Arrow jumps to the edge of the data in one press; the Name Box, left of the formula bar, shows the cell you landed on. Round two: back to the top with Ctrl+Home, then the same trip in one Ctrl+↓ — the Name Box reads A61.
  3. Shift+Arrow grows a selection one cell per press; add Ctrl and the same jump selects to the edge in one press. Take the two dates above with Shift+↑, then the whole date column in one Ctrl+Shift+↑: the selection reads A1:A61.
  4. A workbook is the file and the tabs along the bottom are its sheets: Ctrl+PgDn / Ctrl+PgUp walk them, and the formula bar shows what the active cell really holds. Walk the tabs to Costs and land on the Domain total E4: the grid shows a number, the formula bar shows =B4+C4+D4.
  5. Rename Sheet, Home › Format (Alt, H, O, R), opens the active tab’s name: type the new one and press Enter. Sheet2 is the associate’s inputs scratch — go there and rename it Inputs.
  6. Delete Sheet (Alt, H, D, S) removes the active sheet; Excel asks first, because a deleted sheet cannot be undone. Old wk37 is a dead half-export — delete it and confirm.
  7. Shift+F11 inserts a new worksheet in front of the active one and makes it active. Insert the sheet the weekly report will live on and rename it Report.
  8. Move or Copy Sheet (Alt, H, O, M) moves the active sheet: ↑ ↓ pick where it will sit, Enter confirms. The report reads first: move Report to the front of the workbook.
